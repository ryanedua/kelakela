Southwest's baggage policy page states the limits and then points somewhere else for the money. So this page carries the limits.

Fifty pounds per bag. Sixty-two inches total — length plus width plus height added together, with wheels and handles counted in.

## Bags do not fly free any more

For decades this was the one thing everybody knew about Southwest. It stopped being true in 2025.

The policy page now opens with the words *checked baggage fees apply* and a link to the fee schedule. That link goes to a separate page, which is why no dollar figure appears above: we print what we read on the source, and the source we checked states limits, not prices.

This matters more for Southwest than for any other US carrier. The bags-fly-free era ran long enough that a decade of travel advice, forum answers and listicles assume it. Most of that material is still online, still ranking, and still wrong. If you are reading a Southwest bag guide with no date on it, that is the single most likely thing it has got wrong.

The airline also rebuilt its entire fare structure in January 2026 — Basic, Choice, Choice Preferred, Choice Extra — which invalidated another layer of published advice at the same time.

## Bring a physical credit card

This is the oddity worth knowing, and Southwest is the only major carrier that states it.

If your bag is **overweight or oversized** and you are paying at the airport, Southwest requires a **physical credit card**. Not tap to pay. Not contactless. Not your phone.

The rule is specific to bags that attract weight or size charges at the counter. Standard bag fees paid digitally are unaffected. But if you turn up with a heavy suitcase and nothing in your wallet but a phone, you have a problem that the queue behind you will not enjoy.

## When you can check

Digitally, from twenty-four hours before departure, once you have checked in. Or at a self-tagging kiosk where available, or at the counter with an agent.

## Two things that override the standard policy

**Embargoes.** Southwest restricts oversized, overweight and extra checked bags to some countries. Whether your destination is affected is worth checking before you pack rather than at the counter.

**Partner itineraries.** If your trip involves a partner airline, the operating carrier's baggage policy may govern instead of Southwest's. Same trap as every other carrier: the flight number on your ticket does not always decide the rules your bag travels under.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do bags still fly free on Southwest?**

No. Southwest ended the bags-fly-free policy in 2025 and now charges for checked bags. Its baggage policy page states plainly that checked baggage fees apply. Any guide telling you otherwise was written before the change and has not been updated.

**What are Southwest's size and weight limits?**

Fifty pounds per bag, and 62 inches total for length plus width plus height. Wheels and handles count toward the measurement.

**Can I pay Southwest's overweight bag fee with my phone?**

No. If your bag is overweight or oversized and you pay at the airport, Southwest requires a physical credit card. Tap to pay, contactless and mobile payments are refused for these fees specifically.
