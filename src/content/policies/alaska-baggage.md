The figures above cover Main Cabin and Saver fares travelling within North America, which includes Central America and Hawaii. They are charged per direction, so a round trip with one checked bag each way is ninety dollars rather than forty-five.

## The date that decides your fee is not the date you fly

This is the detail that catches people out, and it is unusual enough to be worth stating plainly: Alaska keys its baggage schedule to **when the ticket was issued**, not when you travel.

The figures on this page apply to tickets issued on or after 10 April 2026. Alaska still publishes two earlier tables — one for tickets issued between 14 October 2025 and 9 April 2026, and one for tickets issued before that. If you booked months ago and fly next week, the older schedule is the one that applies to you.

This is also why fee figures found elsewhere disagree so often. Most of them are not wrong so much as attached to a ticketing window nobody mentioned.

## Hawaii is cheaper

Travel wholly within the state of Hawaii is thirty dollars for a first bag and forty for a second. The forty-five and fifty-five figures are for the rest of North America.

## Several international routes include a free bag

A Main Cabin ticket to Asia or Oceania includes both checked bags at no charge. Europe includes the first bag free, with the second at one hundred dollars. These allowances are not small print — for a long-haul trip they are worth more than the fare difference between cabins on some routes.

## Who does not pay

Atmos Rewards status holders and Atmos Rewards Visa cardholders get a free checked bag, though the card benefit requires that the fare itself was purchased with the card. Business and First fares include two bags at a higher seventy-pound limit. Residents of Alaska and Hawaii have further allowances through Club 49 and Huaka'i.

Alaska is explicit that these benefits do not stack: the highest applicable allowance applies, not the sum of them.

## An Alaska flight number does not guarantee Alaska's rules

Codeshares are the trap here. A flight sold under an Alaska number but operated by Qantas, British Airways, American, Japan Airlines, Finnair, Korean Air, Qatar or one of a dozen others follows the **operating** carrier's baggage policy, not this one. Alaska publishes the affected flight number ranges, and they are extensive.

Check the operating carrier on your confirmation before assuming these figures apply.

## Separate tickets are separate journeys

If your trip spans more than one ticket, bags are checked only as far as the final destination on the first ticket. You collect them, re-check them, and pay the next carrier's fees again. Alaska will through-check across its own and partner flights on a single ticket, but only where the connection is under eighteen hours.
