# Delta eCredit Expiration: Why It's One Year From Purchase, Not Cancellation

The single most common misunderstanding about Delta eCredits is when they expire. Passengers consistently assume the one-year clock starts when they cancel the ticket. It does not. The clock starts when they bought the original ticket — and this distinction can mean the difference between having six months to use a credit versus thinking you have six months and discovering it has already expired. Call **+1-(855)-321-3528)** right now to confirm the exact expiration date on your eCredit before you assume you have time.

## Why the Purchase Date, Not the Cancellation Date

Delta's eCredit validity rule mirrors the standard airline ticket validity framework. A Delta ticket is valid for one year from the date of issue (purchase). When you cancel a non-refundable ticket, the resulting eCredit inherits this same validity window — it does not get a fresh one-year period from the day of cancellation.

The logic from Delta's perspective: you purchased a ticket with a one-year validity. When you cancel, the unused value of that ticket is preserved as a credit. The credit retains the same expiration as the original ticket would have had.

From a passenger perspective, this means the credit clock has been running since the day you bought the ticket, regardless of when you cancelled.

## The Math: How Far the Clock Has Already Run

When you cancel and receive an eCredit, a portion of the one-year window has already elapsed. Here is what that looks like in concrete terms:

Cancel 1 month after purchase: 11 months remain on the eCredit.
Cancel 3 months after purchase: 9 months remain.
Cancel 6 months after purchase: 6 months remain.
Cancel 9 months after purchase: 3 months remain.
Cancel 11 months after purchase: 1 month remains.

This is why passengers who purchase tickets for a trip that is months away and then cancel close to departure can find themselves with very little time to use the eCredit. The calendar months spent between booking and the decision to cancel are already consumed.

## You Only Need to Book Before Expiration

The practical good news is that you do not need to complete your travel before the eCredit expires. You only need to initiate a new booking and complete the purchase before the expiration date. The actual travel can occur after that date.

Delta opens its flight schedule 331 days in advance. This means if you have three months remaining on your eCredit, you can use it today to book a flight departing up to 11 months from now. The credit is consumed when you complete the booking, not when you travel.

Use this to your advantage: even if you have no firm travel plans, booking a flexible Classic or Extra fare on a future date before the credit expires preserves the value. You can change or cancel that booking later, and the eCredit value is locked in.

## Residual Credits After Partial Use

If you use a $400 eCredit to book a $250 flight, Delta issues the remaining $150 as a new eCredit. The new credit carries the same expiration date as the original — it does not receive a fresh one-year window from the date you partially used it.

This means a residual credit can expire very quickly if you are close to the original expiration date. Plan your credit use with this in mind: if you have a large credit approaching expiration, use the full amount in one booking rather than making a partial use and leaving a small residual that will expire soon.

## How to Find Your Exact Expiration Date

**Via My Wallet on delta.com**: Log in to your SkyMiles account, navigate to My Wallet, and select Credits and Certificates. Each eCredit is listed with its value and expiration date.

**Via the Fly Delta app**: Open the app, tap Account, then My Wallet, then Certificates and eCredits.

**Via delta.com/redeem**: If you do not have a SkyMiles account, search for your credit using the 13-digit ticket number (starting with 006, found in your original booking confirmation email), the credit card used to purchase, or your SkyMiles account number. The expiration date appears once the credit is located.

**By phone**: Call **+1-(855)-321-3528)** and provide your ticket number or SkyMiles account. An agent confirms the exact expiration date and current balance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does a Delta eCredit expire one year from purchase or cancellation?**
Purchase date. One year from when you bought the original ticket, not from when you cancelled.

**Why does Delta use the purchase date?**
The eCredit inherits the original ticket's one-year validity period. The cancellation does not reset the clock.

**What if I partially use a Delta eCredit — does the expiration reset?**
No. Residual credits carry the same original expiration date.

**Do I have to travel before my Delta eCredit expires?**
No. You only need to book before expiration. Travel can happen after.

**How do I check my Delta eCredit expiration date?**
My Wallet on delta.com, the Fly Delta app, or call **+1-(855)-321-3528)**.


**Does a Delta eCredit expire one year from the purchase date or the cancellation date?**
One year from the original ticket purchase date — the date you bought the ticket, not the date you cancelled it. If you bought a ticket in March 2025 and cancelled in November 2025, the credit expires in March 2026.

**Why does Delta use the purchase date instead of the cancellation date?**
Delta's standard ticket validity is one year from purchase — the eCredit inherits this validity period from the original ticket. The cancellation doesn't reset the clock because the eCredit represents the value of a ticket that was already purchased with a defined validity window.

**What if I partially use a Delta eCredit — does the expiration date reset?**
No. A residual credit from partial use carries the same original expiration date. If your $300 eCredit expires June 1, 2027 and you use $100 of it in May 2027, the remaining $200 credit also expires June 1, 2027.
