Southwest Airlines passengers travel with a distinct set of protections in 2026 — a combination of federally mandated rights under U.S. DOT rules, voluntary commitments Southwest has made through its Customer Commitment, and unique structural advantages that Southwest's no-change-fee, no-cancellation-fee policy provides beyond what other carriers offer. Understanding exactly what you are owed and how to claim it efficiently is essential, particularly given that Southwest's December 2022 operational meltdown and subsequent DOT settlement have resulted in more explicit public commitments from the carrier about how it handles disruptions. For help exercising your rights right now, call **+1-(855)-321-3191**.

## Your Legally Enforceable DOT Rights on Southwest

The U.S. Department of Transportation establishes minimum passenger protection standards that Southwest must follow as a federally certificated air carrier. These are binding federal regulations with financial penalties for non-compliance.

### Cancellation Refund Rights

When Southwest cancels your flight for any reason — weather, mechanical failure, crew scheduling, or otherwise — you have the right to a **full refund to your original form of payment**. This applies regardless of:

- Whether your ticket was a non-refundable Wanna Get Away or Wanna Get Away Plus fare
- Whether the cancellation was caused by weather or another factor outside Southwest's control
- Whether Southwest offers you a rebooking on an alternative flight
- How far in advance the cancellation was communicated

You are not required to accept a travel voucher, travel funds credit, or Rapid Rewards points in place of a cash refund. The choice between rebooking and a cash refund is entirely yours to make. Refunds must be processed to the original payment method within **7 business days** for credit card purchases and within 20 calendar days for cash or check payments under DOT rules.

This refund right is particularly significant at Southwest because non-refundable Wanna Get Away fares — Southwest's lowest fare tier — would not normally be eligible for a cash refund under voluntary cancellation. A carrier-initiated cancellation overrides the standard fare rules and triggers the full DOT refund entitlement regardless of fare type.

To request a cancellation refund, go to southwest.com under My Trips or call **+1-(855)-321-3191**.

### Significant Delay Refund Rights

As of 2024, U.S. DOT rules extended refund rights to cover significant delays on flights to, from, or within the United States. A significant delay under current DOT rules is defined as:

- **3 hours or more** for domestic flights
- **6 hours or more** for international flights

If your Southwest flight is delayed by these thresholds and you choose not to travel on the delayed flight, you are entitled to a refund of the unused ticket value to your original payment method. This right applies regardless of whether the delay was within Southwest's operational control. Accepting a rebooked alternative flight waives the refund right for that segment.

### Tarmac Delay Protections

DOT regulations impose mandatory time limits on how long Southwest may confine passengers on an aircraft on the tarmac without offering the opportunity to deplane.

**Domestic flights:** Southwest cannot hold passengers on a tarmac-delayed aircraft for more than **3 hours** without returning to the gate and offering deplaning, unless the pilot determines this creates a safety or security risk, or air traffic control advises that returning to the gate would significantly delay the eventual departure.

**International flights arriving at a U.S. airport:** The limit is **4 hours**.

During any tarmac delay exceeding 2 hours, Southwest is required to provide food and water, maintain operable lavatories, and ensure adequate ventilation. These requirements are mandatory under federal law, not optional amenity decisions. Southwest's December 2022 operational failure resulted in a DOT consent order that includes enhanced monitoring of Southwest's operational compliance — making these requirements particularly well-enforced at Southwest in 2026.

### Denied Boarding Compensation

When Southwest oversells a flight and must involuntarily deny boarding to passengers after all voluntary bumping options are exhausted, DOT rules require Southwest to pay **denied boarding compensation**.

**Domestic flights:**
- Arrival delay of 1 to 2 hours: 200% of one-way fare, maximum $775
- Arrival delay exceeding 2 hours: 400% of one-way fare, maximum $1,550

**International flights departing from the U.S.:**
- Arrival delay of 1 to 4 hours: 200% of one-way fare, maximum $775
- Arrival delay exceeding 4 hours: 400% of one-way fare, maximum $1,550

Southwest's open seating model and boarding process reduce the frequency of traditional oversale situations compared to assigned-seat carriers, but denied boarding compensation rights apply whenever involuntary bumping occurs. You are not required to accept a travel voucher — you may request the cash equivalent. Call **+1-(855)-321-3191** if you were involuntarily bumped without a proper compensation offer.

## Southwest's Customer Commitment: Voluntary Protections

Beyond DOT minimums, Southwest maintains a Customer Commitment providing additional protections for disruptions within Southwest's operational control. Following the December 2022 meltdown and DOT consent order, Southwest's Customer Commitment underwent significant revision and includes more specific, enforceable language than prior versions.

Controllable disruptions include mechanical delays, crew scheduling issues, and aircraft substitutions. Weather events, air traffic control restrictions, and security incidents are classified as uncontrollable.

### Meal Vouchers for Controllable Delays

For controllable delays of **3 hours or more at the departure airport**, Southwest provides meal vouchers to affected passengers. These are distributed at the gate or through the Southwest app as digital vouchers redeemable at airport dining concessions.

### Hotel and Ground Transportation for Overnight Controllable Delays

When a controllable disruption causes passengers to be stranded overnight at an airport away from their home city, Southwest's Customer Commitment provides hotel accommodation at a contracted property near the airport and ground transportation to and from the hotel at no charge.

Weather-related overnight disruptions do not trigger this commitment under Southwest's policy. However, given the scrutiny Southwest has faced following 2022, agents have been more proactive about providing goodwill accommodations even in weather scenarios — call **+1-(855)-321-3191** and ask directly if your overnight disruption was caused by a combination of weather and Southwest operational factors.

### Rebooking Commitment

For cancellations and significant delays within Southwest's control, Southwest commits to rebooking affected passengers on the next available Southwest flight at no additional charge. Southwest's no-change-fee policy means this applies automatically — there is no structural change fee to waive because Southwest never charges one.

## Southwest's No-Change-Fee Policy: An Additional Layer of Protection

Southwest's longstanding no-change-fee, no-cancellation-fee policy provides passenger protections that go beyond both DOT requirements and Southwest's own Customer Commitment.

Under DOT rules and most carrier Customer Commitment policies, change fees are waived specifically during declared irregular operations. At Southwest, change fees never apply to any booking under any circumstance — cancellation, disruption, or voluntary change. This means:

**You can rebook to any date without penalty** — including rebooking from a cancelled flight to a departure a week later with no structural fee, regardless of whether Southwest declares the situation an irregular operation.

**Fare differences apply but credits don't expire in the same way** — if you rebook to a lower-priced flight, the difference is held as a travel fund. Southwest travel funds have fixed expiration dates from the original ticket purchase, but the funds themselves are yours to use.

**Wanna Get Away fare holders get refundable protection on cancellations** — the DOT refund right overrides the non-refundable nature of Wanna Get Away fares when Southwest is the party that cancels the flight.

## How to Claim Your Southwest Passenger Rights

**Cancellation refunds:** Submit through southwest.com under My Trips, or call **+1-(855)-321-3191**. If the agent offers travel funds or Rapid Rewards points instead of a cash refund, invoke your DOT cancellation refund right and request the cash equivalent to your original payment method.

**Significant delay refunds:** Submit through southwest.com or call **+1-(855)-321-3191**. Specify that you are invoking your DOT significant delay refund right, provide the delay duration, and confirm your decision not to travel on the delayed flight.

**Denied boarding compensation:** Request immediately at the gate from Southwest's gate agent after the boarding process concludes without you. Ask for the denied boarding compensation calculation based on your one-way fare. Follow up by calling **+1-(855)-321-3191** if not resolved at the airport.

**Meal vouchers and hotel accommodation:** Request from the Southwest gate agent or customer service desk at the airport during the disruption. If not proactively offered for a qualifying 3-hour controllable delay, ask the agent directly whether the delay threshold has been met. Call **+1-(855)-321-3191** to escalate if vouchers are not provided for a qualifying controllable disruption.

**Tarmac delay violations:** If your aircraft was held beyond the 3-hour domestic or 4-hour international limit without the opportunity to deplane, file a complaint with the U.S. DOT at transportation.gov in addition to contacting Southwest.

**U.S. DOT Aviation Consumer Protection Division** accepts formal complaints at transportation.gov. DOT complaints are part of the public enforcement record and are particularly relevant for Southwest given the active DOT monitoring of the carrier's compliance commitments.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What are my rights if Southwest Airlines cancels my flight?**

If Southwest cancels your flight for any reason, U.S. DOT rules entitle you to a full refund to your original payment method regardless of whether your ticket was a non-refundable Wanna Get Away fare. You are not required to accept travel funds or Rapid Rewards points — you may request cash to your original payment method. Alternatively, you may choose rebooking on the next available Southwest flight at no additional charge, with no structural change fee under Southwest's no-change-fee policy. For controllable cancellations, Southwest's Customer Commitment additionally provides meal vouchers for qualifying delays and hotel accommodation for controllable overnight disruptions away from your home city. Call **+1-(855)-321-3191** to request your refund or arrange rebooking.

**Does Southwest Airlines owe me compensation for a significant delay?**

Under U.S. DOT rules expanded in 2024, Southwest must provide refunds for significant delays — 3 hours or more on domestic flights, 6 hours or more on international — when you elect not to travel on the delayed flight. For controllable delays of 3 hours or more at the departure airport, Southwest's Customer Commitment also provides meal vouchers. Unlike the European Union's EC 261/2004 regulation, U.S. rules do not mandate fixed cash compensation purely for delays that fall short of cancellation — but the refund right, meal voucher provision, and potential goodwill compensation apply in qualifying situations. Southwest's post-2022 DOT consent order also makes the carrier more accountable for proactively communicating and delivering these protections. Call **+1-(855)-321-3191** to discuss what applies to your specific delay.

**What are my rights if Southwest involuntarily bumps me from a flight?**

If Southwest involuntarily denies you boarding due to an oversold flight, U.S. DOT rules require cash compensation of 200% of your one-way fare to your final destination (maximum $775) for arrival delays of 1 to 2 hours on domestic flights, and 400% (maximum $1,550) for delays exceeding 2 hours. For international flights departing the U.S., the 200% threshold applies for delays of 1 to 4 hours and 400% for delays beyond 4 hours. You are not required to accept a travel voucher or Rapid Rewards points — you may request the cash equivalent directly from the gate agent. If you were involuntarily bumped without a proper compensation offer, or if the amount appears to be calculated incorrectly, call **+1-(855)-321-3191** before leaving the airport to escalate the issue.
