Alaska Airlines passengers in 2026 travel with a layered set of protections: legally enforceable rights established by the U.S. Department of Transportation, voluntary commitments Alaska has made through its Customer Commitment policy, and contractual rights embedded in every ticket's conditions of carriage. Following the 2025 merger with Hawaiian Airlines and the April 2026 Sabre PSS unification, Alaska's expanded network carries more passengers across more routes than before — making a clear understanding of your rights as an Alaska traveler more relevant than ever. For help claiming compensation or navigating a disruption, call **+1-(855)-321-3341**.

## Your Legally Enforceable DOT Rights

The U.S. Department of Transportation establishes minimum passenger protection standards that Alaska Airlines must follow as a federally certificated air carrier. These are federal regulations with enforcement mechanisms — not voluntary guidelines.

### Cancellation Refund Rights

When Alaska cancels your flight for any reason — weather, mechanical failure, crew shortage, or any other cause — you have the right to a **full refund to your original form of payment**. This right applies regardless of:

- Whether your ticket was purchased as non-refundable
- Whether the cancellation was caused by weather or another factor outside Alaska's control
- Whether Alaska offers you a rebooking on an alternative flight
- How far in advance the cancellation was communicated

You are not required to accept a travel voucher, future flight credit, or Mileage Plan miles in place of a cash refund. The choice between accepting a rebooking and receiving a cash refund is entirely yours. Refunds must be processed to the original payment method within **7 business days** for credit card purchases and within 20 calendar days for cash or check payments under DOT rules.

To request a cancellation refund, go to alaskaair.com under My Trips or call **+1-(855)-321-3341**.

### Significant Delay Refund Rights

As of 2024, U.S. DOT rules extended refund rights to cover significant delays on flights to, from, or within the United States. Under DOT rules, a significant delay is:

- **3 hours or more** for domestic flights
- **6 hours or more** for international flights

If your Alaska flight experiences a delay meeting these thresholds and you choose not to travel, you are entitled to a refund of the unused portion of your ticket to your original payment method. This right applies regardless of whether the delay was within Alaska's operational control. Accepting a rebooked alternative waives the refund right for the affected segment.

### Tarmac Delay Protections

DOT regulations impose hard time limits on how long Alaska may hold passengers on an aircraft on the tarmac without offering the option to deplane.

**Domestic flights:** Alaska cannot hold passengers on a tarmac-delayed aircraft for more than **3 hours** without returning to the gate and offering deplaning, unless the pilot determines this would create a safety or security risk, or air traffic control advises that returning to the gate would significantly delay the eventual departure.

**International flights arriving at a U.S. airport:** The limit is **4 hours**.

During any tarmac delay exceeding 2 hours, Alaska is required to provide food and water, maintain operable lavatories, and ensure adequate ventilation. These are mandatory DOT requirements with significant financial penalties for non-compliance.

### Denied Boarding Compensation

When Alaska oversells a flight and must involuntarily deny boarding to passengers after all voluntary options are exhausted, DOT rules require Alaska to pay **denied boarding compensation** calculated as a percentage of the one-way fare to the final destination.

**Domestic flights:**
- Arrival delay of 1 to 2 hours: 200% of one-way fare, maximum $775
- Arrival delay exceeding 2 hours: 400% of one-way fare, maximum $1,550

**International flights departing from the U.S.:**
- Arrival delay of 1 to 4 hours: 200% of one-way fare, maximum $775
- Arrival delay exceeding 4 hours: 400% of one-way fare, maximum $1,550

These are minimum required amounts. Alaska may offer higher voluntary compensation to solicit volunteers before resorting to involuntary bumping. You are not required to accept a travel voucher in place of cash for involuntary denied boarding. Call **+1-(855)-321-3341** if you were involuntarily bumped without receiving a proper compensation offer.

## Alaska's Customer Commitment: Voluntary Protections

Beyond DOT minimums, Alaska maintains a Customer Commitment providing additional protections for disruptions within Alaska's operational control. Controllable disruptions include mechanical delays, crew scheduling issues, and aircraft substitutions. Weather events, air traffic control restrictions, and security incidents are classified as uncontrollable and do not trigger the voluntary commitment provisions.

### Meal Vouchers for Controllable Delays

For controllable delays of **3 hours or more at the departure airport**, Alaska provides meal vouchers to affected passengers. These are distributed at the gate or through the Alaska app as digital vouchers redeemable at airport dining concessions.

### Hotel and Ground Transportation for Overnight Controllable Delays

When a controllable disruption causes passengers to be stranded overnight at an airport away from their home city, Alaska's Customer Commitment provides hotel accommodation at a contracted property near the airport and ground transportation to and from the hotel at no charge.

Weather-related overnight disruptions do not trigger this commitment under Alaska's policy, consistent with DOT regulations that do not require hotel accommodation for weather events.

### Rebooking on Controllable Disruptions

For cancellations and significant delays within Alaska's control, Alaska commits to rebooking affected passengers on the next available Alaska flight at no additional charge with waived change fees. Following the April 2026 Sabre unification, agents can also rebook across the combined Alaska and Hawaiian network — including widebody transpacific flights — for passengers whose original itinerary cannot be reconstructed on Alaska narrowbody metal alone.

## How to Claim Your Alaska Airlines Passenger Rights

**Cancellation refunds:** Submit through alaskaair.com under My Trips, or call **+1-(855)-321-3341**. If the agent offers a travel voucher or Mileage Plan miles instead of a cash refund, invoke your DOT cancellation refund right and request the cash equivalent to your original payment method.

**Significant delay refunds:** Submit through alaskaair.com or call **+1-(855)-321-3341**. Specify that you are invoking your DOT significant delay refund right, provide the delay duration, and confirm your decision not to travel on the delayed flight.

**Denied boarding compensation:** Request immediately at the gate from Alaska's gate agent after the boarding process concludes without you. Ask the agent directly for the denied boarding compensation calculation based on your one-way fare. Follow up by calling **+1-(855)-321-3341** if the matter is not resolved at the airport.

**Meal vouchers and hotel accommodation:** Request from the gate agent or customer service desk at the airport during the disruption. If not proactively offered for a qualifying 3-hour controllable delay, ask the agent whether the delay threshold has been met and request the voucher. Call **+1-(855)-321-3341** to escalate if vouchers are not provided for a qualifying controllable disruption.

**Tarmac delay violations:** If your aircraft was held beyond the 3-hour domestic or 4-hour international limit without the opportunity to deplane, file a complaint with the U.S. Department of Transportation at transportation.gov in addition to contacting Alaska.

## Filing a Formal Complaint

If Alaska does not honor your rights through standard service channels, two formal escalation options are available.

**Alaska's formal complaint process** is initiated through alaskaair.com under the Customer Care section. Written complaints receive a formal response from Alaska's customer relations team.

**U.S. DOT Aviation Consumer Protection Division** accepts formal complaints at transportation.gov. DOT complaints are part of the public enforcement record and contribute to regulatory oversight of carrier compliance.

For immediate assistance with any passenger rights situation before escalating formally, call **+1-(855)-321-3341**.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What are my rights if Alaska Airlines cancels my flight?**

If Alaska cancels your flight for any reason, U.S. DOT rules entitle you to a full refund to your original payment method regardless of whether your ticket was non-refundable. You are not required to accept a travel credit or Mileage Plan miles — you may request cash to your original payment method. Alternatively, you may choose to be rebooked on the next available Alaska flight at no additional charge. For controllable cancellations, Alaska's Customer Commitment additionally provides meal vouchers for qualifying delays and hotel accommodation for controllable overnight disruptions away from your home city. Following the April 2026 Sabre unification, rebooking now spans the combined Alaska and Hawaiian network. Call **+1-(855)-321-3341** to request your refund or arrange rebooking.

**Does Alaska Airlines owe me compensation for a significant delay?**

Under U.S. DOT rules expanded in 2024, Alaska must provide refunds for significant delays — 3 hours or more on domestic flights, 6 hours or more on international — when you elect not to travel on the delayed flight. For controllable delays of 3 hours or more at the departure airport, Alaska's Customer Commitment also provides meal vouchers. Unlike the European Union's EC 261/2004 regulation, U.S. rules do not mandate fixed cash compensation purely for delays that don't reach cancellation. However, the refund right, meal voucher provision, and potential goodwill compensation apply in qualifying situations. Call **+1-(855)-321-3341** to discuss what protections apply to your specific delay.

**What are my rights if Alaska Airlines involuntarily bumps me from a flight?**

If Alaska involuntarily denies you boarding due to an oversold flight, U.S. DOT rules require cash compensation of 200% of your one-way fare to your final destination (maximum $775) for arrival delays of 1 to 2 hours on domestic flights, and 400% (maximum $1,550) for delays exceeding 2 hours. For international flights departing the U.S., the 200% threshold applies for delays of 1 to 4 hours and 400% for delays beyond 4 hours. You are not required to accept a travel voucher — you may request the cash equivalent. If you were involuntarily bumped without a proper compensation offer, or if the amount offered appears incorrect, call **+1-(855)-321-3341** before leaving the airport to escalate the issue.
