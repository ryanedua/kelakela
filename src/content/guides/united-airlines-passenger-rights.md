United Airlines passengers travel with a layered set of protections in 2026: federal rights established by the U.S. Department of Transportation, voluntary commitments United has made through its Customer Commitment policy, and contractual rights embedded in every ticket's fare rules. Knowing which protections apply to your specific situation — and how to claim them effectively — is the difference between accepting an unfavorable outcome and receiving what you are legally and contractually owed. For help claiming compensation or navigating a disruption, call **+1-(844)-578-2245**.

## Your Legally Enforceable DOT Rights

The U.S. Department of Transportation establishes minimum passenger protection standards that all U.S. carriers including United must follow. These are federal regulations with enforcement mechanisms, not voluntary commitments.

### Cancellation Refund Rights

When United cancels your flight for any reason — weather, mechanical issue, crew shortage, or otherwise — you have the right to a **full refund to your original form of payment**. This right applies regardless of:

- Whether your ticket was labeled non-refundable
- Whether the cancellation was caused by factors outside United's control
- Whether United offers you a rebooking on an alternative flight
- How far in advance the cancellation occurs

You are not required to accept a travel credit, future flight voucher, or MileagePlus miles in place of a cash refund. The choice between accepting a rebooking and receiving a refund belongs entirely to you. Refunds must be processed to the original payment method within **7 business days** for credit card purchases and within 20 calendar days for cash or check payments.

To request a cancellation refund, go to united.com under My Trips or call **+1-(844)-578-2245**.

### Significant Delay Refund Rights

As of 2024, U.S. DOT rules extended refund rights to significant delays on flights to, from, or within the United States. A significant delay is defined as:

- **3 hours or more** for domestic flights
- **6 hours or more** for international flights

If your United flight experiences a delay meeting these thresholds and you choose not to travel, you are entitled to a refund of the unused portion of your ticket to your original payment method. This right applies regardless of whether the delay was within United's control. You must actively choose not to travel and request the refund — accepting a rebooked flight waives this right for the affected segment.

### Tarmac Delay Protections

U.S. DOT regulations impose hard time limits on how long United may keep passengers on an aircraft on the tarmac without offering the opportunity to deplane.

**Domestic flights:** United cannot hold passengers on a tarmac-delayed aircraft for more than **3 hours** without deplaning, unless the pilot determines that deplaning poses a safety or security risk, or air traffic control advises that returning to the gate would significantly delay departure.

**International flights arriving in the U.S.:** The limit is **4 hours**.

During any tarmac delay exceeding 2 hours, United is required to provide food and water to passengers, maintain operable lavatories, and ensure adequate cabin ventilation. These requirements are mandatory under DOT rules, not discretionary.

### Denied Boarding Compensation

When United oversells a flight and involuntarily denies boarding to passengers, DOT rules require United to pay **denied boarding compensation** calculated as a percentage of the one-way fare to the passenger's final destination.

**Domestic flights:**
- Arrival delay of 1 to 2 hours: 200% of one-way fare, maximum $775
- Arrival delay exceeding 2 hours: 400% of one-way fare, maximum $1,550

**International flights originating in the U.S.:**
- Arrival delay of 1 to 4 hours: 200% of one-way fare, maximum $775
- Arrival delay exceeding 4 hours: 400% of one-way fare, maximum $1,550

These amounts are minimums. United may offer higher voluntary compensation to solicit volunteers before resorting to involuntary bumping. You are not required to accept a travel voucher in place of cash for involuntary denied boarding — you may request the cash equivalent. Call **+1-(844)-578-2245** if you were involuntarily bumped without receiving proper compensation.

## United's Customer Commitment: Voluntary Protections

Beyond DOT minimums, United maintains a Customer Commitment providing additional protections for disruptions within United's control. Controllable disruptions include mechanical delays, crew scheduling issues, and aircraft substitutions. Weather events, air traffic control restrictions, and security incidents are not considered controllable.

### Meal Vouchers for Controllable Delays

For controllable delays of **3 hours or more at the departure airport**, United provides meal vouchers to affected passengers. These are typically distributed at the gate or issued as digital vouchers through the United app redeemable at airport concessions.

### Hotel and Ground Transportation for Overnight Controllable Delays

When a controllable disruption causes passengers to be stranded overnight at an airport away from their home city, United's Customer Commitment provides hotel accommodation at a contracted property near the airport and ground transportation to and from the hotel at no cost to the passenger.

These accommodations are for controllable overnight disruptions only. Weather-related overnight stranding does not trigger the hotel commitment under United's policy, consistent with industry standards and current DOT regulations.

### Rebooking on Controllable Disruptions

For cancellations and significant delays within United's control, United commits to rebooking affected passengers on the next available United flight at no additional charge, with waived change fees for subsequent modifications within the disruption window. In exceptional international disruption circumstances, agents may rebook onto Star Alliance partner flights when United metal is not available within an acceptable timeframe.

## How to Claim Your United Passenger Rights

**Cancellation refunds:** Submit through united.com under My Trips, or call **+1-(844)-578-2245**. If the agent offers a travel credit instead of a cash refund, reference your DOT cancellation refund right and request the cash equivalent to your original payment method.

**Significant delay refunds:** Submit through united.com or call **+1-(844)-578-2245**. Specify that you are invoking your DOT significant delay refund right, provide the delay duration, and confirm your decision not to travel on the delayed flight.

**Denied boarding compensation:** Request at the gate from United's gate agent immediately after the boarding process concludes. If you are displaced without a compensation offer, ask the agent directly for the denied boarding compensation calculation based on your one-way fare. Follow up by calling **+1-(844)-578-2245** if the issue is not resolved at the airport.

**Meal vouchers and hotel accommodation:** Request from United's gate agent or customer service desk during the disruption. If not proactively offered for a qualifying controllable delay, ask the agent specifically whether the delay meets the 3-hour controllable threshold and request the voucher. Call **+1-(844)-578-2245** to escalate if vouchers are not provided for a qualifying disruption.

**Tarmac delay violations:** If your aircraft was held on the tarmac beyond the 3-hour domestic or 4-hour international limit without the opportunity to deplane, you can file a complaint with the U.S. Department of Transportation at transportation.gov in addition to contacting United.

## Filing a Formal Complaint

If United does not honor your rights after you raise them through standard channels, two formal escalation paths are available.

**United's formal complaint process** is initiated through united.com under the Customer Care section. Written complaints receive a formal response within the timeline United specifies in its Customer Commitment.

**U.S. DOT Aviation Consumer Protection Division** accepts formal complaints about DOT rule violations at transportation.gov. DOT complaints become part of the public enforcement record and contribute to DOT oversight actions against carriers with pattern violations.

For immediate assistance with any passenger rights issue before escalating to a formal complaint process, call **+1-(844)-578-2245**.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What are my rights if United Airlines cancels my flight?**

If United cancels your flight for any reason, U.S. DOT rules entitle you to a full refund to your original payment method regardless of whether your ticket was non-refundable. You are not required to accept a travel credit or MileagePlus miles — you may request cash to your original payment method. Alternatively, you may choose rebooking on the next available United flight at no additional charge. For controllable cancellations — mechanical issues, crew scheduling — United's Customer Commitment additionally provides meal vouchers for delays of 3 hours or more and hotel accommodation for controllable overnight disruptions away from your home city. Call **+1-(844)-578-2245** to request your refund or to be rebooked on an alternative flight.

**Does United owe me compensation for a long delay?**

Under U.S. DOT rules expanded in 2024, United must provide refunds for significant delays — defined as 3 hours or more on domestic flights and 6 hours or more on international flights — when you choose not to travel on the delayed flight. For controllable delays of 3 hours or more at the departure airport, United's Customer Commitment also provides meal vouchers. Unlike the European Union's EC 261/2004 regulation, U.S. rules do not mandate fixed cash compensation purely for delays that fall short of cancellation — but the refund right, meal voucher provision, and potential goodwill compensation from agents apply in qualifying situations. Call **+1-(844)-578-2245** to discuss what applies to your specific delay.

**What compensation does United owe if I am involuntarily bumped?**

If United involuntarily denies you boarding due to an oversold flight, U.S. DOT rules require cash compensation of 200% of your one-way fare to your final destination (maximum $775) for arrival delays of 1 to 2 hours on domestic flights, and 400% (maximum $1,550) for delays exceeding 2 hours. For international flights departing the U.S., the 200% threshold applies for delays of 1 to 4 hours and 400% for delays beyond 4 hours. You are not required to accept a travel voucher — you may request the cash equivalent. If you were involuntarily bumped without a proper compensation offer, or if the amount offered appears incorrect based on your one-way fare, call **+1-(844)-578-2245** immediately before leaving the airport to escalate the issue.
