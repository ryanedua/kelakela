# How Long Does a Delta eCredit Last? Expiration Rules for 2026

The answer is one year — but it is one year from the original ticket purchase date, not from when you cancelled. This distinction trips up a significant number of passengers who believe their credit is younger than it actually is. If you bought a ticket in January 2025 and cancelled in October 2025, your eCredit expired in January 2026 — three months after you cancelled. The cancellation date is irrelevant to the expiration calculation. Call **+1-(855)-321-3528** to confirm the exact expiration date on your specific credit before you lose access to it.

## The One-Year Rule: How It Works

Delta eCredits are valid for exactly one year from the date your original ticket was issued. The word "issued" here means the date of purchase — when you bought the ticket, not when you travelled on it, not when you cancelled it, not when the eCredit was generated.

Here is a concrete example: You buy a Delta ticket on March 10, 2025 for a July 4, 2025 trip. In June 2025 your plans change and you cancel. Delta issues you an eCredit. That eCredit expires on March 10, 2026 — the anniversary of your original purchase. You have less than nine months to use it, even though you just cancelled.

This is why passengers are surprised when credits expire. The expiration runs from a date that may feel like ancient history by the time you try to use the credit.

## You Only Need to Book — Not Travel — Before Expiration

Here is the most practically useful thing to know about eCredit expiration: you do not need to complete your travel before the credit expires. You only need to book a new flight before the expiration date.

Delta publishes its flight schedule 331 days in advance. This means if your eCredit expires in three months, you can use it today to book a flight departing ten months from now. As long as you initiate the booking before the expiration date, the credit is consumed and locked into your new ticket. The travel itself can happen well after the expiration date.

This gives you a practical way to preserve credit value even when you have no immediate travel plans: book a refundable Classic or Extra fare as a placeholder on a future date, then modify or cancel later when your actual plans are clearer. The eCredit is used and protected once the booking is confirmed.

## Residual Credits After Partial Use

When you apply an eCredit toward a flight that costs less than the full credit value, Delta issues the remaining balance as a new eCredit. The critical rule: the residual credit carries the same original expiration date as the credit you used, not a fresh one-year window.

Example: You have a $300 eCredit expiring April 1, 2027. You book a $100 flight in March 2027. The remaining $200 is reissued as a new eCredit also expiring April 1, 2027. You now have one month to book again before it expires.

Plan your credit use with this in mind. If a large credit is approaching expiration, use it all in one booking or book a more expensive placeholder rather than leaving a small residual that will expire quickly.

## How to Check Your eCredit Expiration Date

**Via your SkyMiles account**: Log in to delta.com, go to your profile, and navigate to My Wallet. Under Credits and Certificates, each eCredit is listed with its value and expiration date.

**Via the Fly Delta app**: Open the app, tap Account, then My Wallet, then Certificates & eCredits.

**Via delta.com/redeem**: If you do not have a SkyMiles account, search for your credit using the 13-digit ticket number from your original booking confirmation email (starts with 006) or the credit card used to purchase.

**By phone**: Call **+1-(855)-321-3528** with your booking confirmation number or SkyMiles account. An agent can confirm the exact expiration date and current balance.

## What to Do If Expiration Is Approaching

**Book a placeholder flight**: Use the credit to book any Classic or Extra fare on a future date before expiration. You can change or cancel this booking later without a fee. The act of booking consumes the credit and locks in its value.

**Call Delta proactively**: Call **+1-(855)-321-3528** before the credit expires and ask about options. Delta occasionally considers extensions for extenuating circumstances. Calling before expiry is significantly more effective than calling after.

**Check if the credit should have been cash**: If the credit originated from a Delta-cancelled flight or a qualifying significant schedule change, you may have been entitled to a cash refund rather than an eCredit. Call **+1-(855)-321-3528** to review the original cancellation reason — credits from qualifying events may be convertible to cash even now.

## Credits With Longer Than 1-Year Validity

In standard 2026 policy, all voluntarily cancelled non-refundable tickets produce eCredits with 1-year validity from purchase date. The only current exception involves eCredits issued to passengers who experienced qualifying DOT-defined disruptions between May 16 and October 27, 2024. Those specific credits were extended to 5 years from the disruption date under DOT compliance requirements.

If you believe your credit originated from a qualifying 2024 disruption, call **+1-(855)-321-3528** to check whether the 5-year extension applies to your specific ticket.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does a Delta eCredit last?**
One year from the original ticket purchase date — not the cancellation date.

**Do I have to fly before my Delta eCredit expires?**
No. Book before expiration — travel can happen after.

**Does the expiration date reset when I partially use a Delta eCredit?**
No. Residual credits keep the same original expiration date.

**Can Delta extend an eCredit past 1 year?**
Not as standard policy. Call **+1-(855)-321-3528** before expiration to ask. Results are case by case.

**Are there Delta eCredits with longer than 1-year validity?**
Yes, for qualifying 2024 DOT disruptions, which were extended to 5 years. All others are 1 year.


**Can Delta extend an eCredit past the 1-year expiration?**
Not as standard policy in 2026. Extensions are considered case by case at Delta's discretion. Call +1-(855)-321-3528 before expiration to ask. Calling proactively before the credit expires gives better results than calling after.
