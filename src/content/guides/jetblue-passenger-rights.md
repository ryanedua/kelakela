JetBlue Airways passengers in 2026 travel with a notably stronger set of protections than passengers on most U.S. carriers. In addition to the legally enforceable rights established by the U.S. Department of Transportation, JetBlue maintains a **Customer Bill of Rights** — one of the most specific and passenger-friendly voluntary compensation frameworks in the U.S. airline industry — that provides automatic travel credits for controllable delays and cancellations beyond what DOT rules require. Understanding both layers of protection and how to claim them is the difference between accepting an inconvenient outcome and receiving what you are actually owed. For help exercising your rights right now, call **+1-(855)-234-9895**.

## Your Legally Enforceable DOT Rights on JetBlue

The U.S. Department of Transportation establishes minimum passenger protection standards that JetBlue must follow as a federally certificated air carrier. These are binding federal regulations enforced through DOT audits and financial penalties.

### Cancellation Refund Rights

When JetBlue cancels your flight for any reason — weather, mechanical failure, crew scheduling, or otherwise — you have the right to a **full refund to your original form of payment**. This right applies regardless of:

- Whether your ticket was a non-refundable Blue Basic, Blue, Blue Plus, Blue Extra, or Mint fare
- Whether the cancellation was caused by weather or a factor outside JetBlue's control
- Whether JetBlue offers you a rebooking on an alternative flight
- How far in advance the cancellation was communicated

You are not required to accept a travel credit or TrueBlue points in place of a cash refund. The election between accepting a rebooking and receiving a cash refund is entirely yours. Refunds must be processed to the original payment method within **7 business days** for credit card purchases and within 20 calendar days for cash or check payments under DOT rules.

To request a cancellation refund, go to jetblue.com under Manage Trips or call **+1-(855)-234-9895**.

### Significant Delay Refund Rights

As of 2024, U.S. DOT rules extended refund rights to cover significant delays on flights to, from, or within the United States. A significant delay under current DOT rules is:

- **3 hours or more** for domestic flights
- **6 hours or more** for international flights

If your JetBlue flight is delayed by these thresholds and you choose not to travel, you are entitled to a refund of the unused ticket value to your original payment method. This applies regardless of whether the delay was within JetBlue's operational control. Accepting a rebooked alternative waives the refund right for that affected segment.

### Tarmac Delay Protections

DOT regulations impose mandatory time limits on how long JetBlue may confine passengers on an aircraft on the tarmac without offering the opportunity to deplane.

**Domestic flights:** JetBlue cannot hold passengers on a tarmac-delayed aircraft for more than **3 hours** without returning to the gate and offering deplaning, unless the pilot determines this creates a safety or security risk, or air traffic control advises that returning to the gate would significantly delay the eventual departure.

**International flights arriving at a U.S. airport:** The limit is **4 hours**.

During any tarmac delay exceeding 2 hours, JetBlue is required to provide food and water, maintain operable lavatories, and ensure adequate ventilation. These requirements are mandatory under federal law — not optional amenity decisions.

### Denied Boarding Compensation

When JetBlue oversells a flight and must involuntarily deny boarding to passengers after all voluntary bumping options are exhausted, DOT rules require JetBlue to pay **denied boarding compensation** calculated as a percentage of the one-way fare to the final destination.

**Domestic flights:**
- Arrival delay of 1 to 2 hours: 200% of one-way fare, maximum $775
- Arrival delay exceeding 2 hours: 400% of one-way fare, maximum $1,550

**International flights departing from the U.S.:**
- Arrival delay of 1 to 4 hours: 200% of one-way fare, maximum $775
- Arrival delay exceeding 4 hours: 400% of one-way fare, maximum $1,550

You are not required to accept a travel credit in place of cash compensation for involuntary denied boarding. Call **+1-(855)-234-9895** if you were involuntarily bumped without a proper compensation offer.

## JetBlue's Customer Bill of Rights: Beyond DOT Minimums

JetBlue's Customer Bill of Rights is one of the most detailed voluntary passenger protection frameworks offered by any U.S. carrier. Unlike most airline Customer Commitment policies that describe general obligations, JetBlue's Bill of Rights specifies **automatic compensation amounts** tied to specific delay thresholds for controllable disruptions — meaning you are entitled to these credits without having to negotiate or escalate.

### Automatic Compensation for Controllable Departure Delays

For delays at the departure gate caused by factors within JetBlue's control — mechanical issues, crew scheduling, aircraft substitutions — JetBlue's Customer Bill of Rights provides automatic travel credits based on delay length:

- **Delays of 1 to 1 hour 59 minutes:** $50 travel credit
- **Delays of 2 to 2 hours 59 minutes:** $100 travel credit
- **Delays of 3 to 3 hours 59 minutes:** $150 travel credit
- **Delays of 4 hours or more:** $200 travel credit

These credits are in addition to, not instead of, the DOT refund right for significant delays. A 3-hour controllable delay entitles you to both the $150 travel credit under the Bill of Rights and the DOT refund right if you choose not to travel.

### Automatic Compensation for Controllable Cancellations

For flights cancelled due to factors within JetBlue's control, the Customer Bill of Rights provides an automatic travel credit in addition to the DOT refund right. If JetBlue cancels your flight for a controllable reason and you are not rebooked on an alternative flight departing within 4 hours of the original scheduled departure, you are entitled to:

- A full refund or rebooking at your option (DOT right)
- A travel credit based on the length of the cancellation delay (Bill of Rights addition)

### Meal Vouchers for Controllable Delays

For controllable delays of **3 hours or more** at the departure gate, JetBlue provides meal vouchers or snack boxes to affected passengers. These are distributed at the gate or through the JetBlue app as digital vouchers.

### Hotel and Ground Transportation for Overnight Controllable Delays

When a controllable disruption causes passengers to be stranded overnight at an airport away from their home city, JetBlue's Customer Bill of Rights provides hotel accommodation at a contracted property near the airport and ground transportation at no charge.

Weather-related overnight stranding does not trigger the hotel accommodation commitment under JetBlue's Bill of Rights, consistent with current DOT regulations.

## How JetBlue's Bill of Rights Differs From Other Carriers

Most U.S. carriers describe their Customer Commitment in general terms — "we will provide meal vouchers for controllable delays" — without specifying dollar amounts or automatic disbursement mechanisms. JetBlue's Customer Bill of Rights is different in two important ways.

**Specific dollar amounts** are stated for each delay threshold rather than vague descriptions of "compensation" or "goodwill gestures." You know exactly what you are entitled to before you contact JetBlue.

**Automatic disbursement** is the stated mechanism. Travel credits under the Bill of Rights are supposed to be issued automatically to your TrueBlue account for qualifying controllable disruptions, without requiring you to file a claim or negotiate with an agent. If you experienced a qualifying controllable delay and did not receive the applicable credit, call **+1-(855)-234-9895** to request the credit be applied.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What are my rights if JetBlue cancels my flight?**

If JetBlue cancels your flight for any reason, U.S. DOT rules entitle you to a full refund to your original payment method regardless of fare type — including non-refundable Blue Basic fares. You are not required to accept a travel credit or TrueBlue points. Alternatively, you may choose rebooking on the next available JetBlue flight at no charge with waived change fees. For controllable cancellations, JetBlue's Customer Bill of Rights additionally provides an automatic travel credit if the rebooking departure is more than 4 hours after the original scheduled time, plus meal vouchers for qualifying delays. Call **+1-(855)-234-9895** to request your refund, arrange rebooking, or verify that your Bill of Rights travel credit was issued correctly.

**Does JetBlue owe me compensation for a significant delay?**

Yes, and JetBlue's compensation framework for controllable delays is more specific than most U.S. carriers. Under JetBlue's Customer Bill of Rights, controllable departure delays trigger automatic travel credits: $50 for delays of 1 to 1:59 hours, $100 for 2 to 2:59 hours, $150 for 3 to 3:59 hours, and $200 for delays of 4 hours or more. These credits are disbursed automatically to your TrueBlue account for qualifying controllable disruptions. In addition, U.S. DOT rules provide a refund right for significant delays of 3 hours or more on domestic flights and 6 hours or more on international flights when you choose not to travel. Meal vouchers also apply for controllable delays of 3 hours or more at the departure gate. Call **+1-(855)-234-9895** to verify your Bill of Rights credit was applied or to discuss compensation for your specific delay.

**What are my rights if JetBlue involuntarily bumps me from a flight?**

If JetBlue involuntarily denies you boarding due to an oversold flight, U.S. DOT rules require cash compensation of 200% of your one-way fare to your final destination (maximum $775) for arrival delays of 1 to 2 hours on domestic flights, and 400% (maximum $1,550) for delays exceeding 2 hours. For international flights departing the U.S., the 200% threshold applies for delays of 1 to 4 hours and 400% for delays beyond 4 hours. You are not required to accept a travel credit — you may request the cash equivalent directly from the gate agent. If you were involuntarily bumped without a proper compensation offer, or if the amount appears calculated incorrectly based on your one-way fare, call **+1-(855)-234-9895** before leaving the airport to escalate the issue with an agent.
